## Main obligations when redistributing

When you distribute the Work or a derivative version, Apache 2.0 requires important attribution/compliance steps.

In practical terms:

1. Include a copy of the Apache License 2.0.
2. Keep relevant copyright, patent, trademark and attribution notices.
3. Mark files you modified with prominent notices stating that you changed them.
4. If the original distribution includes a `NOTICE` file, include the relevant NOTICE attribution in an allowed location.
5. Do not represent your modifications as being officially endorsed by the original project unless you have permission.

Read Section 4 of [LICENSE](../LICENSE) for the controlling redistribution requirements.

## Trademarks and the Coreor name

The software license and the project brand are separate concepts.

Apache 2.0 does **not** give a general right to use project trademarks, trade names, logos or product branding. The license permits reasonable/customary use needed to describe the origin of the software and reproduce NOTICE attribution, but it does not grant broad trademark rights.

Accordingly:

- you may accurately say your product is based on or derived from Coreor Database,
- you should not imply that an independent fork/service is the official Coreor service,
- use of Coreor logos or branding may require separate permission depending on the use.

See Section 6 of [LICENSE](../LICENSE) and the repository [NOTICE](../NOTICE).

## Hosting a modified version

Apache 2.0 does not contain an AGPL-style network-copyleft requirement.

That means a person or company can generally run a modified version as a hosted service without being required by Apache 2.0 itself to publish every private modification merely because users access it over a network.

Other legal obligations may still apply depending on how software is distributed and which third-party dependencies are included.
